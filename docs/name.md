# Where the name comes from

Mynaphone is a myna plus a gramophone. The bird listens, and the gramophone plays it back. You say it
MY-nuh-fone, like the bird.

## A bird that repeats what it hears

The word myna [comes from Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myna), *mainā*, and mynas are known as
talking birds. In captivity, the [hill myna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_hill_myna) can learn
to copy human speech and everyday sounds.

Mynaphone does that job for your music. It listens to what your PC plays and keeps a copy of every
song it hears all the way through, so you can hear it again later.

The bird in the app's icon is the [common myna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_myna), also
called the Indian myna. If you've spent any time in an Indian town, you've almost certainly met one.
It has a brown body, a black head, a bright yellow beak and a bare yellow patch behind the eye.

That eye works as the app's status light. It stays dark while Mynaphone listens, and it turns red
only while a song is recording.

## Records that play without a signal

A gramophone plays music from flat records.
[Emile Berliner's gramophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonograph) couldn't record anything, and
the old wind-up ones didn't even need a power socket.

Songs in your Mynaphone library work like those records. Once a song is in, it plays whether or not
the internet is working, on your PC, your phone or the USB stick in your car.

*Gramophone* [comes from 2 Greek words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonograph#Terminology),
*gramma*, meaning a letter, and *phōnē*, meaning voice, so it's roughly a written voice. Mynaphone
writes down what it hears too, as a tagged file for each song.

## Spelled with or without an h

You'll also see the bird written as *mynah*. The app's name uses the shorter spelling.
